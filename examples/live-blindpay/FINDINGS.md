# What a BlindPay development instance actually did

Runs of `yarn workspace live-blindpay start` and a few one-off probes, on 2026-11-11, against one BlindPay **development** instance and the public Stellar testnet. A development instance is not production: treat each answer as strong evidence, and keep the adapter options until BlindPay confirms in writing.

Ids, keys and personal data are left out on purpose. Accounts are named by role.

## The open questions

| Question | What the development instance did | What covers it |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Who submits the signed transaction? | **Both work.** With `partner_submits` we never sent the envelope, yet its hash was on testnet, successful, about 5 s after `POST /payouts/stellar`: BlindPay submitted it. With `client_submits` (grid-stellar submitted first) BlindPay took the create for a transaction already on the ledger (`tracking_transaction.status: "found"`). | `runStellarPayout` `submission`. The default `client_submits` stays: it confirms the move on chain before BlindPay hears of it. |
| (b) Is `signed_transaction` the envelope or a hash? | **The signed envelope, base64 XDR.** A 64-hex hash was refused with `400 REQUEST_REJECTED`, message `transaction_xdr_invalid`, even for a transaction already on the ledger. That probe ran once, by hand: the 10 USDB it sent stayed with BlindPay with no payout, so the example does not repeat it. | Adapter `signedTransactionField`: the default `signed_xdr` is right. Do not use `tx_hash`. |
| (c) Time bounds and fee of the authorize XDR | One `payment`, fee **100 stroops**, `minTime` 0, **`maxTime` about 30 s after the authorize call**, no memo, no other preconditions, while the quote still had about 300 s left: `maxTime` is always well before the quote expiry and `assertPayoutXdrMatches` accepts it. A signature made after `maxTime` is refused at create (`400 REQUEST_REJECTED`, message `submit_transaction_failed`) and no payout is made; the flow stops with `PayoutIncompleteError` at `create`, and grid-stellar proves `expired_not_landed` from Horizon. Authorize answers again for the same quote: it does not consume it. | No package change. The key holder has about 30 s, not the quote's 5 minutes; see the recommendations. |
| (d) Which account does a payout pay? | **The USDB issuer itself** (the account the trustline XDR names): payouts on development are a payment back to the issuer, that is a burn. Every authorize transaction paid it: the same account across the 6 quotes of each run, three runs, and the one-off probes. | Adapter `payoutDestination`. The example reads `BLINDPAY_PAYOUT_DESTINATION`, or uses the USDB issuer with a FINDING line. Production pays an account BlindPay must give you in writing. |
| (e) Is a fee-bumped envelope accepted? | **Yes.** We signed the payment once and wrapped it in a fee bump paid by the same test account; BlindPay submitted it and completed the payout. Its `tracking_transaction.transaction_hash` is the **outer** (fee-bump) hash. | `readLeg`'s `txHash` is that outer hash; grid-stellar's submit and lookup already find a fee bump by both hashes. |
| (f) Reading one payout | `GET /payouts/{po_id}` works. Statuses seen: `completed`, `failed`, `refunded`. On development the create already answers with the final status. | The adapter's path was right; its constant is no longer marked unconfirmed. |
| (g) Quote lifetimes | Payin and payout quotes: `expires_at` **300 s** after the request (epoch milliseconds). | Adapter `maxQuoteTtlMs` (default one hour) accepts them. |

## Other differences from the docs

1. **Stellar payins never completed.** Every PIX payin to a Stellar testnet wallet (about ten, across four wallets, sentinel amounts included) stayed `processing` as long as we watched, over 27 minutes for the oldest: `tracking_transaction` and `tracking_payment` reached `completed` within about 30 s, `tracking_complete` stayed `processing` with no hash, and no USDB arrived on testnet. The docs say every development payin completes in about 30 s. So the payin sentinel outcomes and the delivery check on testnet could not be observed; the example reports this as a FINDING and checks that nothing arrived on chain either. The instance's own seeded payins on an EVM test network do show `completed`, `failed` and a delivery hash.
2. **The USDB trustline needs authorization.** The USDB issuer is `auth_required`. The trustline from `create-asset-trustline` stays unauthorized (grid-stellar `checkReceiveReadiness`: `trustline_not_authorized`) until a `mint-usdb-stellar` call, which authorizes it and mints. `create-asset-trustline` answers `{ success, xdr }`: one `changeTrust` with a `maxTime` 300 s out.
3. **Where a leg's hash is.** A payout has the hash in `tracking_transaction` and repeats it in `tracking_complete`. On the instance's seeded EVM payins the delivery hash is in `tracking_complete.transaction_hash`, and `tracking_transaction` tracks the PIX and has `external_id`, no hash. No Stellar payin ever completed (item 1), so that shape is **unconfirmed for Stellar**. **Package fix:** `readLeg` and `parseWebhook` read a payin's hash from `tracking_complete` first and keep the fallback to `tracking_transaction`, with regression tests on the EVM shape.
4. **Failed and refunded payouts keep the tokens.** With 66600 and 77700 as the payout quote's `request_amount`, the payout ended `failed` and `refunded` as documented, but BlindPay had already submitted our transaction and the USDB had left the account. Nothing came back on testnet for the refunded one within 30 s (the docs mention a real refund transaction for EVM payouts only).
5. **Payout quotes carry a `contract` on Stellar.** The docs say `null`; the development answer holds an EVM token approval (ABI, amount), irrelevant to a Stellar payout.
6. **Two error body shapes.** Most errors are `{ success, code, description, message, errors, trace_id }`: `code` is a generic class (`VALIDATION_INVALID_REQUEST`, `REQUEST_REJECTED`, `QUOTES_RATE_UNAVAILABLE`), and the specific reason is only in `message` (`transaction_xdr_invalid`, `submit_transaction_failed`, `beneficiary_name_must_match_customer_for_first_party`, `fx_rate_unavailable`). Validation errors have no top-level `code`: `{ success, message, errors: [{ code, message, path }], trace_id }`. The client keeps a code-shaped `code` as `partnerCode` (undefined for validation errors) and drops free text unless `includeErrorBodies` is on.
7. **Payout minimum.** A payout quote below `request_amount` 500 (5.00) is refused as a validation error; the docs say 10 USD.
8. **Wallets and ownership.** A Stellar wallet takes the direct-address flow only (`is_account_abstraction: true`); no signature or proof of ownership was asked. The create answers 200 with `customer_id` added.
9. **Quotes and fees.** A `sender` payin quote of 100.00 BRL gave 17.77 to 17.78 USDB with `flat_fee: 10` (USDB cents); the payin adds `transaction_fee_amount`. A 10 USDB payout quote gave 9.59 USD with `flat_fee: 40`. A `receiver` payin quote was once refused (`QUOTES_RATE_UNAVAILABLE`, `fx_rate_unavailable`) and priced later the same day.
10. **Bank accounts.** A `first_party` ACH account must carry the customer's name; the example uses `vendor_or_supplier` with clearly fake details.
11. **Rate limit.** No `429` and no rate-limit headers at about 30 requests a minute.
12. **Webhooks.** Not delivered live: there is no public URL. Signature checks stay covered by the offline tests.

## Calls the package does not wrap

The example makes these through the client's own `http.request` (same auth, idempotency key, timeout and redaction), never with raw `fetch`: read the instance, list customers, list and create bank accounts, `create-asset-trustline`, `mint-usdb-stellar`, delete a wallet. Bank accounts are needed for every payout, so they are the first worth wrapping.

## Recommendations left open

These touch a public API or a safety rule, so they are written down instead of changed:

1. **Deprecate `signedTransactionField: 'tx_hash'`** once BlindPay confirms in writing: development refuses a hash, and trying moves the payment without a payout.
2. **The authorize transaction's 30 s window.** `runSignedPayout` checks the quote's expiry, not the transaction's `maxTime`, and `assertPayoutXdrMatches` accepts a transaction whose `maxTime` has passed. Nothing unsafe follows (it cannot land, and BlindPay refuses it), but a slow key holder (`custody: 'client'`) ends in `PayoutIncompleteError` at `create`, then `needs_attention`. Consider refusing to sign once `maxTime` has passed, and size the submitter's `timeoutMs` plus `maxWaitMs` to about 30 s for BlindPay.
3. **`partner_submits` and a refused create.** When BlindPay refuses the create, the flow cannot tell whether BlindPay tried to submit. The transfer goes to `needs_attention`; a person checks the hash on Stellar, and the sequence proof decides when a replacement may be signed. Keep it that way; no automatic retry.
4. **Error reason.** Consider exposing BlindPay's `message` as its own field when it is a lowercase snake_case token, so callers can branch on `transaction_xdr_invalid` without `includeErrorBodies`. Free text must still never become the code.
5. **Ask BlindPay** why Stellar payins stay `processing` on development, and for the production payout account in writing.
